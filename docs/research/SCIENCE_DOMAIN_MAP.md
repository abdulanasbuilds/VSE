# Science Domain Map

Purpose: keep the research search broad while choosing a narrow product slice. This map is an index of fields and research questions, not a complete account of science or an endorsement that the linked sources cover every subfield.

## Domain inventory

| Domain | Concepts/examples worth making understandable | Sources to investigate first | Special quality risks | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | arithmetic, geometry, probability, statistics, algebra, calculus, optimization, discrete math, number theory, logic, proofs, open problems | OpenAI math, arXiv, Lean community, GeoGebra, Desmos, Wolfram Demonstrations | A visualization may illustrate examples without proving a theorem; theorem scope and proof status matter | CANDIDATE; detailed OpenAI math audit pending |
| Computer science | algorithms, data structures, complexity, distributed systems, compilers, databases, networks, security concepts | original papers/repos, arXiv, official docs, interactive algorithm explainers | benchmark results can be selection-biased; implementation behaviour differs from theory | NOT STARTED |
| AI and machine learning | tokens, embeddings, neural networks, attention, training, inference, evaluation, interpretability, multimodal systems | original papers, official technical reports, reproducible code/data, OpenAI science | simplified neural-network diagrams can be mistaken for a literal internal trace; model claims need eval details | PARTIAL SOURCE DISCOVERY |
| Physics | mechanics, energy, waves, electromagnetism, thermodynamics, relativity, quantum mechanics, particle physics | NASA, CERN Open Data, PDG, INSPIRE-HEP, arXiv, PhET | scales, idealized assumptions, measurement limits, and probabilistic results are easy to misrepresent | PARTIAL SOURCE DISCOVERY |
| Astronomy and space | orbits, stars, galaxies, exoplanets, cosmology, space missions, planetary science | NASA SVS/interactives, NASA ADS, ESA science, mission data | images may be colour-mapped, composites, artist impressions, or transformed data; label correctly | PARTIAL SOURCE DISCOVERY |
| Chemistry and materials | atoms, molecules, reactions, bonding, equilibrium, kinetics, materials, catalysts, nanotechnology | PubChem, Materials Project, papers, Wolfram demonstrations | reaction diagrams simplify electron behaviour; predicted properties aren't experimentally confirmed facts | PARTIAL SOURCE DISCOVERY |
| Biology | cells, genetics, evolution, ecology, physiology, molecular mechanisms, proteins | AlphaFold, UniProt, RCSB PDB, NCBI, Europe PMC, experimental papers | correlation versus causation; predicted structures vs experimental evidence; model organism limits | PARTIAL SOURCE DISCOVERY |
| Medicine and public health | anatomy, disease mechanisms, diagnostics, epidemiology, intervention evidence | PubMed, PMC, WHO, systematic reviews, official health agencies | high-stakes; require conservative wording, current clinical evidence, and no unsupported personal advice | CANDIDATE; elevated review bar |
| Earth and climate science | weather, climate, oceans, tectonics, water cycle, glaciers, ecosystems, natural hazards | NASA Earthdata/SVS, NOAA, USGS, Copernicus | time ranges, uncertainty, spatial resolution, model projection vs observation | PARTIAL SOURCE DISCOVERY |
| Engineering | structures, circuits, robotics, control systems, energy systems, transport, manufacturing | standards, university labs, original papers, manufacturer/agency data | safety margins and real-world operating conditions; simplified models can be unsafe outside stated range | CANDIDATE |
| Environmental science | biodiversity, pollution, carbon cycles, land use, conservation, resource systems | OWID, UNEP, NASA, NOAA, USGS, official monitoring data | conflicting definitions, missing measurements, geographic limitations, changing datasets | PARTIAL SOURCE DISCOVERY |
| Economics and social science | inequality, demography, migration, development, incentives, networks, social systems | Our World in Data, World Bank, UN, OECD, official statistical agencies, papers | causal claims, definitions, survey bias, country comparability, political framing | PARTIAL SOURCE DISCOVERY |
| History and archaeology of science | history of concepts, instruments, discoveries, competing hypotheses, timelines | academic publishers, museums, institutional archives, primary historical sources | later narratives may oversimplify attribution or chronology; check original evidence | NOT STARTED |
| Earth observation and geospatial | map projections, remote sensing, terrain, geographic processes, urban systems | NASA Earthdata, Copernicus, USGS, national statistics and mapping agencies | map projections, scale, sampling, privacy and location sensitivity | CANDIDATE |
| Statistics and evidence literacy | uncertainty, distributions, sampling, regression, Bayes, hypothesis testing, evidence synthesis | open datasets, OWID, research methods papers, statistical education tools | p-hacking, base-rate neglect, misleading axes, confidence intervals vs probability interpretations | CANDIDATE |
| Information and complex systems | networks, feedback, emergence, dynamical systems, chaos, cellular automata, information theory | Wolfram Demonstrations, original papers, Observable and computational notebooks | attractive patterns are not necessarily evidence of the proposed causal mechanism | CANDIDATE |
| Linguistics and cognition | language, perception, memory, learning, decision making, human-computer interaction | primary papers, open research datasets, educational research | avoid presenting contested psychological theory as settled or universal | NOT STARTED |

## Cross-disciplinary layers to record for every topic

- What phenomenon or question is being explained?
- What system boundary and level of abstraction is in scope?
- What is directly observed, formally proven, inferred, simulated, predicted, or hypothesized?
- What are the units, time range, spatial range, assumptions, and uncertainty?
- Which sources are primary, secondary, official data, independent interpretation, or educational examples?
- What visual representation is appropriate and what could it misleadingly suggest?
- What is the learner expected to understand or be able to test?
- Which facts or resources were not found yet?

## Expansion rule

Prioritize topics that have strong source quality, a clear mental-model gap, an honest visualization path, manageable scope, and a measurable learning outcome. Do not prioritize an area solely because it is fashionable or has a large amount of searchable content.
